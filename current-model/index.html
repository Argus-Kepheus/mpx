In development: Gleeble tests and models

Multiphysics is being refactored to accommodate physical-simulation tests performed on Gleeble equipment and to develop computational models and simulations of those tests. The aim is to reuse the platform’s thermoelectric core beyond flash-butt welding.

The initial focus is the thermoelectric response under prescribed electrical excitation, including specimen geometry, free span and heat exchange with the jaws. Calculated temperatures will be compared with thermocouple measurements. Machine-control strategies follow plant validation across heating, flashing and upsetting.

This is work in progress. Reproducing Gleeble tests and assessing the models against experimental measurements still require implementation, traceable datasets and comparison criteria defined in advance.

The 1D numerical baseline covers FDM, FEM and fixed-geometry FVM, with independent GNU Octave, C++17 and Julia implementations verified on the declared reference cases. Project-current performance is measured under the governed P1/P2 campaign, within its recorded cases, machine and toolchains. Cross-engine post-processing and reproducibility contracts are in place. The predictive Maturity-1 Flashing closure is implemented opt-in for FDM and FEM and numerically verified under ADR 0035 (I1–I4 accepted on 2026-10-21), not calibrated, not validated. Since ADR 0036 (accepted on 2026-10-22) it also runs on FVM with partial front control volumes and converges to the FDM/FEM limit. It starts from a cold interface: every realized bridge rupture removes material; the solidus enthalpy gate is diagnostic only. Maturity 0 remains the default Flashing path. Reference closure parameters are analogue values, not established DP600 or CSN/NMW values. The Upsetting formulation remains open; on FVM, Maturity-0 Flashing and geometry-changing Upsetting/Pressing remain unpromoted. Numerical agreement does not establish experimental validation, which remains future work.
