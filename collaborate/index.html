Experimental collaboration

Experimental data to understand welding

We welcome welding researchers who can share measurements or technical feedback to help develop, calibrate and validate future physical models. Software and architecture work continue independently of receiving data or replies.

Research in progress

How does upsetting affect the joint temperature?

In flash-butt welding, we want to measure the compression work from force and displacement, and assess how deformation concentrated near the joint affects temperature. This investigation is ongoing.

  • Synchronized signals. Force, displacement, current and voltage series on the same time axis, covering the end of flashing and the upsetting stage. If available, include units, stage markers and how displacement was defined or corrected (for example, machine travel or specimen shortening).
  • Joint shape or removed flash. A measured geometric profile or a scaled image of the joint before flash trimming, or the mass of the removed flash.
  • A link to the same weld. A common identifier connecting the signals, profile, image or flash mass to the same weld.

Force and displacement help estimate the mechanical work; current and voltage help identify electrical heating that may still be present. Joint shape or flash mass helps assess how the material deformed.

Partial or anonymized data can also help; a complete dataset is not required to start a conversation. Tell us what you can share.

Scientific exchange

Other ways to contribute

Beyond upsetting, these model and data questions can guide a collaboration:

  • Do you have numerical time–temperature series, with the measurement location and method?
  • Which machine settings and boundary conditions are essential to interpret a welding test?
  • Which measurements best assess a reduced flash-butt-welding model when direct interface temperatures are unavailable?
  • Which temperature-dependent material properties and phase-transformation data should the material library prioritize?

Confidentiality and industrial constraints

Some relevant datasets may be owned by companies, protected by contractual confidentiality, or constitute industrially sensitive information and therefore cannot be disclosed. No researcher is expected to violate those obligations, and sharing raw proprietary data is not a prerequisite for contributing.

A critical technical assessment is also highly valued: send the project lead comments on model assumptions, missing physics, measurement methods or relevant literature.

Data ownership, redistribution rights and publication permissions are treated independently. Scientific use does not automatically imply permission to publish raw data.

Contributions may lead to formal project participation and, where the scientific contribution justifies it, co-authorship of resulting publications.
